## The finding that matters, and that is NOT repaired
**The election-loser data cut against the clean version of the domain test.** Elections have a named winner, a recorded vote and a certification, yet conspiracism rises among losers after them (Miller et al., 2025; Wang et al., 2022; Kim et al., 2022). The chapter's counter-case says clear-attribution domains produce anger and lawsuits rather than conspiracism. The loser data do not fit that without a further move.

Available reply: the conspiracy targets election *administration* (thousands of counties, machines, rules), which is distributed, not the winner, who is named. That reply is plausible and is not yet tested. A coded-corpus check is the test: do post-election claims name the distributed apparatus or the named winner?

A second, harder point: winners' conspiracy belief falls after a win (Kim et al., 2022). Agent-supply can say control was restored, but so can the plain loss-of-control account, so this observation does not discriminate between them.

## Result
- P5 survives as a claim about **where the reach lands and what shape it takes**, not about why it starts. The chapter's wording ("a rational response to a real structural feature") should keep the word *reach* and avoid claiming the cause.
- Added to Ch. 6: one paragraph placing P5 beside agency detection, control deprivation and coalition detection (strengthening found, so it enters the text per the weaknesses rule).
- Not added to the text (no strengthening yet): the elections counter-case. Appendix D already carries the domain test; extend it only after the coded-corpus check.
